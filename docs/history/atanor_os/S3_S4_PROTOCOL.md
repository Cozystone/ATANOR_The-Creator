## Founder decision 2026-11-25: "Yes, add empathy" - a resonance bedrock with another of its kind
Grounding (human and animal): emotional contagion is the oldest layer of empathy (de Waal's Russian doll; Preston & de Waal 2002 perception-action
model): seeing a conspecific's state moves the observer's own state, before any understanding. Rats and mice learn where danger or food is from
a conspecific's distress or eating (observational fear: Jeon et al. 2010; social transmission of food preference: Galef). It needs only
(1) a cue of the other's state that the senses carry, and (2) the observer's own state being moved by it, felt where the observer is.
The bedrock, fixed now (the only new evolved part):
- Sense (the body declares it, like a distress call or a visible expression): when another of its kind is in view (not behind a wall) within
  6 cells, the observer receives that body's inside changes this beat (food, water rises and falls); out of view, nothing.
- Resonance (the bedrock): a seen rise of another's inside is felt as a small relief of the same inside, weight .3, where the observer itself
  stands - its own going organ remembers it as relief in its own place unit (the same memory as its own relief, a third as strong).
Nothing else changes: no rule to follow, no imitation of gestures, no reward for nearness. Following, if it comes, comes from the observer's own
going toward where it felt (resonant) relief. Harm resonance (another's death felt as harm here) is the next step, one change at a time.

# S3b (registered before running; founder 2026-11-25, after a review: "the bottleneck is not the world model but context separation")
Question: can the being's own memory tell "this is another world" from "I am wrong", from its own failed expectations - no new bedrock, no
eye-made label, no symbol. This is the first direct test of self marker 1 (own error vs the world's change).
Theory: latent-cause inference (Gershman, Blei & Niv 2010; Gershman & Niv 2012; renewal of extinguished conditioning, Bouton 2004): memories
are not overwritten when the world changes; a run of large prediction errors is read as a new cause, and a new memory is opened; when the old
world comes back its memory comes back with it. In S3 the being had one relief memory per place and could only unlearn slowly (half-life ~1,400
beats against thousands of reliefs): B 9102 (sides swapped) failed the whole stay.
Mechanism (atanor_os/emerge/going.py, ctx=K): the place units and their chain are shared; what was relieved where is held per context (up to 4).
Each beat each held context, and a blank one that knows only how often reliefs come at all, is scored by how well it foretold where reliefs came
and did not (Bernoulli log-likelihood at each body's place unit, summed over bodies, fading .99 a beat). When another beats the one in use by 20,
memory moves to it; a blank winning opens a new context. Only the context in use learns and fades; the others are kept as left.
Runs: the two S3 sequences again (a: 9101 9102 9103 9101; b: 9104 9105 9106 9104), ctx=4, everything else as S3.
Bars: (a) new rooms: first-1,500 deaths <= .3 in all four new-room stays (S3: 1.32, 1.00, .01, 0) and last need fills >= 3 in all four;
(b) return: first-1,500 deaths <= .1 in both, and the context in use on return is the one the first stay ended with; (c) forks >= .6 at the end
of every stay. Marker (world vs own error): in seq a a new context opens in B within 1,000 beats, C (A's sides) takes back A's context rather
than a new one; within any stay at most one switch after the first 1,000 beats (spring depletion must not read as a new world).
Readings: all met -> the being separates worlds from its own failed expectations (stage 3 done; marker 1 measured). (a) met but the marker
not (switches all over) -> it works by churning memory, not by telling worlds apart; report. (a) not met with the right switches -> the
mind's other memories (value) still carry the old world; next is context for them too.

# S3d (registered before running; founder 2026-11-25: "break the barrier - do not believe the present experience at once")
Theory: in S3b-S3c2 the memory in use learned each observation in the same step that should have judged it, so a new world was absorbed
before it could count against the old one. In latent-cause inference an observation is first assigned to a cause, then learned; people meeting
the unexpected hold it ("that's odd"), then decide "another place" or "I was wrong", then learn.
Mechanism (Going hold=300, contexts scored by possible crossings as S3c2): a crossing the map in use has never seen is held out of it (the
newcomer learns it). If within 300 beats the newcomer wins, it is another world and the held crossings go with the new context; if not, the map
in use was wrong and takes them in (a "merge", counted). While its place units are still being born (its first world young), it learns at
once. Runs: S3 baseline (no contexts) and S3d, each sequence a and b, two starts (rep 0, 1) - 8 runs, so that chance between runs is seen.
Bars for S3d (same as S3b, judged on both starts): new rooms first deaths <= .3 in at least 7 of 8 new-room stays; return first deaths <= .1
and the return's context = the first stay's in 3 of 4; forks >= .6 at stay ends in 14 of 16; churn: switches after a stay's first 1,000
beats <= 1 in 14 of 16 stays. Also reported: merges ("I was wrong") per stay, against switches ("another world").
Amended before any S3d run (founder 2026-11-25: faster): no fresh baseline runs - the two baseline starts per sequence are S3 and S3c2 (S3c2
never switched, so it ran as plain S3; caveat: it carried the context bookkeeping). S3d: 4 runs at once (2 sequences x 2 starts). A quick
