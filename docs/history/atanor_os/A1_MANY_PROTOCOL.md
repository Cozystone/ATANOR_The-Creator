# D20: is it the amount of experience? One mind in 64 bodies (registered 2026-11-24, before running)

## Why
D19 (first 3 layouts): in the smallest room the being does alternate between the springs (up to 6 + 6 need-directed visits, 0 deaths per 3,000 beats),
but nothing carries as the room grows - at full size it lives as before (0-1 visits, 3-4 deaths). All of A1 gave the mind ONE body for 12,000 beats
(about 40 minutes of its time); the being's main line lives as one mind in 512 bodies (founder 2026-11-22). 12,000 transitions is very little for
learning place x need by experience.

## Question (a clean split, not an adoption)
With 64 times the experience in the same world, does this mind - as it is, nothing changed - come to choose by need?
- yes -> the organs can learn it; the open problem is efficiency (how to get there with less: memory, movement units), measured against this ceiling.
- no  -> the organs themselves cannot learn it from experience; the design is the break.

## Design
Same being file (atanor.pt), cued depletion T room at full size, 12,000 beats, 64 bodies living at once with one mind (each reborn at the start cell when
it dies), dev 9101-9103 x mirror. Compared with the one-body lives already recorded (a1cache_cues, A1 v3 numbers).
Measured over the life in four quarters (3,000 beats each), per body: need-directed fills (a fill of the store that was the lower one), all fills, deaths;
the share of beats the value decided and its earned trust. Then the A1 v2 fork with the life's own value (first match, recovery). (Amended before running: the offline relief arm is left out here -
the question is about the being's own value, and the many-body ring needs per-body links the offline tool does not have yet.)
## Reading (fixed now)
Learning within the life: deaths per body in the last quarter at most half of the first quarter AND need-directed fills per body at least double;
fork: first match >= .65 and recovery >= .40 for the life's own value. Both -> yes. Only the life improves -> it learns to live but the fork does not show it
(then the fork's start state is examined). Neither -> no.
